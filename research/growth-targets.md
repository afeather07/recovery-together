## The one already-existing dedicated organization in this exact space

**[7-HOPE Alliance](https://7hopealliance.org/)** — a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit whose entire mission is 7-OH public education, science, user support, and advocacy. This is the single closest-matched potential partner found: not a general addiction nonprofit that happens to mention kratom, an organization built specifically around 7-OH. Worth understanding their positioning before any outreach — early signals suggest an emphasis on "safe, legal access" and harm reduction rather than abstinence-only framing, which may or may not fully align with Recovery Together's peer-support-for-people-stopping focus; read their actual site and materials before drafting anything, not just this summary.

## Researchers

**Dr. Kirsten Smith (Johns Hopkins)** — the most-cited name in current kratom/7-OH withdrawal research; her team's academic work specifically studied real-world quitting experiences via r/quittingkratom and r/OPMS (the same subreddits in `research-C`'s Reddit plan), and she appears as a recurring expert voice (e.g., an April 2026 ASAM Practice Pearls episode). If Recovery Together ever wants research-backed credibility or a future case-study/data relationship, she's the most relevant single person identified. [Johns Hopkins coverage](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/news/articles/2025/02/emerging-use-of-kratom-in-us-kratom-use-disorder-experts-propose-decision-making-algorithm-for-clinicians)

**Dr. Michele Ross** — addiction neuroscientist who has spoken publicly on kratom/7-OH (an SSDP-hosted webinar). Secondary lead, not as directly kratom-focused as Dr. Smith.

## Professional / institutional bodies already publishing on this topic

- **ASAM** (American Society of Addiction Medicine) — has produced clinician-facing kratom/7-OH education (podcast + practice materials). Not a peer-to-peer fit, but relevant if Recovery Together ever wants a clinical-credibility citation or referral relationship.
- **Partnership to End Addiction** (drugfree.org) — large, established national nonprofit, already publishing a 7-OH explainer. A bigger, more institutional target than 7-HOPE Alliance; a partnership here would carry more weight but is also a much longer relationship-building process for a solo, pre-launch founder.
- **Students for Sensible Drug Policy (SSDP)** — drug-policy/harm-reduction advocacy nonprofit, already hosted 7-OH-specific programming. Harm-reduction framing may be a closer philosophical match than abstinence-only orgs.
- **OD Free Marin** — a local (Marin County) harm-reduction org with its own 7-OH explainer content — an example of the kind of smaller, regional harm-reduction orgs likely to exist elsewhere too; worth a broader pass later, not exhaustively researched here.

## Podcasts

**[Kratom Sobriety Podcast](https://kratomsobriety.com/)** ([Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/kratom-sobriety/id1685569424)) — exists specifically to fill "the lack of formal and accessible places for problematic kratom users to find effective help." This is the single most directly aligned podcast found — same audience, same problem statement, arguably a natural cross-promotion or guest-appearance fit once there's a real product and some usage data to talk about.

**The Curbsiders** (#533, "What's the Tea on Kratom?") — a general medical-education podcast for clinicians that did one kratom episode. Lower priority: clinician audience, not peer/patient audience, one-off topic rather than ongoing focus.

**ASAM Practice Pearls** (kratom/7-OH episode, April 2026, hosting Dr. Kirsten Smith) — same clinician-audience caveat as above.

## Organic search gap (adds to `research-C`'s SEO section, doesn't replace it)

The clearest current pattern in search results for "7-OH"/"kratom withdrawal" queries: heavy dominance by (a) case-report-style clinical/academic content (PubMed, ScienceDirect) written for clinicians, not people in withdrawal, and (b) paid-treatment-center content (the same category `recoverytogether.com`'s lead-gen site falls into, per `launch-checklist.md`). Very little exists that is: written in plain language, for the person going through it (not a clinician reading about them), non-commercial, and not gated behind a "give us your insurance info" form. That gap is exactly what the Recovery Library already shipped in V1.1 is positioned to fill — this is validation that the content strategy already underway is aimed at a real, underserved gap, not a general assumption.
